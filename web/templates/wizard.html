Run a test

One session = one (body, lens, display) triple, about 15 minutes once set up. Work through the steps in order. Everything here is from the capture protocol.

Pick your gear

Any Apple display works, an iPhone screen is fine. It's about mass production: every display is calibrated differently, but Apple mass-produces tightly calibrated panels, so it's the closest thing to everyone photographing the exact same picture.

Shoot RAW (add camera-JPEG too if you want the body's processing look — it's a separate, first-class measurement class). No tripod needed — handheld is fine. Registration warps every shot back onto the target pixel-for-pixel, so framing and tilt don't matter. Just take your clearest shot.

Treat it like an event. Plan every body + lens combo you want to measure, gather them all, then take one windowless room, kill the lights, put an Apple device on, and run the combos back to back. One evening gets you several profiles.

Display prep (do once, don't touch again)

  1. True Tone OFF, Night Shift OFF, auto-brightness OFF (macOS, iOS, iPadOS alike). Measured, not theoretical: True Tone shifted a display white ~6% redder at the sensor and drifts with ambient light — it silently breaks comparability.
  2. Brightness to a fixed notch, roughly 2/3. Note the notch. Never change it mid-session.
  3. Show each frame at exactly 1:1 native pixels, fullscreen, no UI. The viewer in step 4 handles this.
  4. Let the panel warm up ~15 min before the first frame (backlight drift).
Dark room. No windows leaking, no LEDs. Tape over camera and monitor status lights. Wear dark clothes — you reflect.

Camera setup

  • RAW (+JPEG). Base ISO. White balance LOCKED to any fixed preset (note which). Never auto-WB. Manual exposure. Handheld, so leave IBIS/OIS on.
  • Aperture: wide open (lowest f-number). That's where a lens shows its character — vignette, glare, soft corners, and especially the bokeh you get on the points frame. By f/8 most lenses converge on their best behaviour and hide it. Want a stopped-down profile too? That's a second session, not a stricter first one. Phones and fixed-lens compacts: skip this, the camera decides.
  • Focus by magnified live view on a corner marker, then switch to MF and don't touch.
  • Get all four corner markers in the frame with a little margin — that's what registration needs. Framing and tilt don't matter beyond that; closer (more pixels per stimulus pixel) is more confidence.
  • On the tone frame, expose so the 255 white block sits just under clipping (blinkies barely off). Bracket freely from there — see the shot list.
  • Keep shutter ≥ 1/30s (OLED/PWM). In a dark room that's easy handheld.

Shot list

Per lens. Tick as you go. Bracket freely — take a bunch of shots per frame at different shutter speeds until the set feels repeatable and complete. Ingestion keeps the best take per frame automatically and tells you what it rejected, so multiple takes are good, not a mistake.

    Focus-lock trick for the points frame. AF hunts on a black field with sparse dots. Do not add room light. Acquire focus on the previous bright frame (flat/edges), lock it (long-press AE/AF lock, or manual focus), then cycle to points without refocusing. The display distance is unchanged, so the lock is exact.

    Then a darkness check: one exposure with the display showing pure black. Anything visible is light leak — fix the room. When you're done, add a photo of your rig in the last step, then upload.
